# The Midnight Train — paper prototype (3 beats)

**How to play by text/call:** One of you is the **Bold One**, the other the **Careful One** (pick now).
For each beat: read the SCENE aloud (or paste it in chat). The prompted player writes their answer
in their own words — 1 to 3 sentences. Then the OTHER player (playing **Fate**) picks one of the
three consequences — without negotiating. Read the consequence aloud. Swap roles. Continue.

The story remembers: when Fate picks an option marked 🔖, note the mark on paper. It pays off
later — one already pays off in beat 3.

---

## BEAT 1

**SCENE**

The 11:58 sleeper pulls out exactly on time, which is the last normal thing it does.

You two found the platform by accident — a stairway in the station that the map insists is a wall.
Now you're settled into a velvet compartment as the city lights smear past, and the conductor
appears in the doorway: tall, silver-buttoned, with a ticket punch shaped like a crescent moon.

"Tickets," he says. You have one. Between the two of you. One.

His eyebrow suggests he has been doing this job for a very, very long time, and that this
particular situation is his favorite kind.

**PROMPT → the Bold One:**
*One ticket, two passengers, and a conductor waiting. What do you say?*

> ✍ *(Bold One writes 1–3 sentences)*

**FATE → the Careful One picks how the world answers:**

1. **The conductor laughs** — a sound like a brass bell — and punches the ticket once.
   "That's the best answer I've had in a decade. Ride on. I'll be watching you two with
   *interest*." 🔖 *mark: CONDUCTOR-AMUSED*
2. **The conductor tears the ticket cleanly in half** and hands one piece to each of you.
   "Half a fare each. The Line will decide who owes the difference. It always does."
   🔖 *mark: TICKET-HALVED*
3. **The conductor raises his lantern** to your faces and goes still. "Huh," he says, in the
   tone of a man recognizing someone from a photograph. He punches the ticket without
   another word and leaves quickly. 🔖 *mark: LANTERN-LOOKED*

---

## BEAT 2

**SCENE**

Past midnight, the windows stop showing the world.

The train glides through dark country, and then — light. A station slides by that should not
exist: warm lamps, a bench, a name on the sign that the **Careful One** knows. It looks exactly
like a place you miss. A place, or a time, or a someone.

The train is not slowing. But it isn't hurrying either. Trains on this line, you begin to
suspect, listen.

**PROMPT → the Careful One:**
*The window is showing you somewhere you miss. Tell your companion what you see — and what
you'd do if the train stopped.*

> ✍ *(Careful One writes 1–3 sentences)*

**FATE → the Bold One picks how the world answers:**

1. **The station passes, and your companion takes your hand** as it goes. Some places are
   for visiting. Some are for carrying. The compartment feels warmer after.
   🔖 *mark: CARRIED-TOGETHER*
2. **The train slows.** Audibly. Deliberately. For five terrible seconds it *offers* — and
   then glides on, leaving you to live with what you almost did. 🔖 *mark: THE-OFFER*
3. **A voice from the corridor:** an old passenger in a moth-soft coat, watching the same
   window. "I got off at mine, once," she says. "Don't." Then she's gone down the corridor.
   🔖 *mark: THE-WARNING*

---

## BEAT 3

**SCENE**

At 3:03 the train stops at a station that has never been printed on any timetable. The sign
says **PROVISIONAL**. The platform is empty except for a Stationmaster with a ledger the size
of a door, who steps aboard and walks directly to your compartment, as though your names are
already in the book. They might be.

"One ticket. Two passengers." The ledger opens. A nib scratches. "The Midnight Line runs on
fairness, not money. One of you owes a fare, and fares on this line are paid in *true things*.
A confession. A promise. A first memory. Something real."

The pen hovers. The train waits. Trains on this line, you now know for certain, listen.

**PROMPT → the Bold One:**
*The fare must be something true. What do you offer?*

> ✍ *(Bold One writes 1–3 sentences)*

**FATE → the Careful One picks how the world answers:**

1. **The Stationmaster writes it down slowly, nodding.** "Accepted. Overpaid, in fact." A
   stamped receipt is handed over: *CREDIT — ONE STOP, ANY STATION, INCLUDING THE ONES THAT
   AREN'T.* 🔖 *mark: FARE-CREDIT*
2. **The ledger flips backward on its own** — pages and pages — and stops. "Mm. That has
   been offered before. Nineteen-twelve. The Line remembers everything. Offer deeper."
   *(The Bold One must write one more sentence — and this one costs.)* 🔖 *mark: LEDGER-TESTED*
3. **Before the Stationmaster can answer, the old passenger from the corridor steps in** and
   lays a worn token on the ledger. "Their fare's mine. I've carried spares for forty years —
   waiting for someone worth spending one on." *(Only available if you hold THE-WARNING or
   THE-OFFER — otherwise pick again.)* 🔖 *mark: THE-PASSENGER'S-DEBT*

---

**END OF PROTOTYPE.** *(The full story would run ~12–15 beats: where the Line actually goes,
what the conductor recognized in your faces, the passenger's story, and a last stop where the
marks you collected decide what the train asks of you — and what it offers.)*

**After playing, ask yourselves:** Did writing feel fun or like homework? Did playing Fate feel
mischievous or mean? Did either of you peek ahead? (Peeking ahead is a *good* sign.)
